§ 01

Executive summary

DealsGate is a protected-deal platform for Nigerian social commerce. It is not a marketplace catalogue. It is a transaction gateway that sits between a seller who already sells on Instagram or WhatsApp and a buyer who is willing to pay — if they can be sure the item is real, the money is safe, and the delivery is tracked.

This brief accompanies a complete, self-contained MVP demonstration of the platform. The demo bundle consists of eleven HTML files, each a fully interactive scenario-driven simulator of one part of the product. Together, they cover every screen, every state transition, and every policy branch required by the FINAL 1.0 master specification.

The bundle is not a mock-up. It is a working reference — every demo runs on real numbers, real state machines, and real audit trails. Buyers pay into an escrow ledger. Sellers deposit a ₦5,000 guarantee. Vet Agents earn and withdraw. Admins mediate disputes. Every action leaves a durable, timestamped trace.

§ 02

The problem we are solving

Nigeria's social commerce economy is one of the largest in Africa. Millions of small businesses sell electronics, fashion, home appliances, and vehicles through Instagram, WhatsApp, and TikTok. There is no shortage of inventory. There is no shortage of demand. The bottleneck is trust.

What buyers fear

  • Pay-before-delivery scams. The seller disappears after receiving payment, or ships a counterfeit, defective, or entirely different item.
  • No recourse. Once money is sent by direct bank transfer, there is no mechanism to reverse it. Police reports are slow. Court recovery is impractical for the amounts involved.
  • Fake social proof. Instagram follower counts, screenshots, and testimonials are cheap to fabricate. There is no independent third party verifying a seller's actual transaction history.

What sellers fear

  • Ghost buyers. Order placed, item dispatched, buyer unreachable at delivery. Return shipping is on the seller.
  • Fake payment alerts. Screenshots of bank transfers that were never made, or were reversed after dispatch.
  • Buyer disputes after delivery. A buyer claims the item was different from what was listed, with no evidence either way. The seller has no defence.

Why existing solutions do not work

Traditional marketplaces (Jumia, Konga) solve for trust by centralising the transaction — but they require sellers to list on a catalogue and to comply with platform logistics. Social sellers do not want that. They have built their brands on their own channels; they want the freedom of Instagram plus the protection of a real escrow.

Escrow-only services protect the money but not the item. The buyer still pays for an item they have never seen, in a condition they cannot verify, shipped by a seller whose history they cannot check. The trust gap is only half-closed.

The gap

No existing Nigerian platform combines regulated escrow, mandatory physical inspection by a certified agent, and publicly verifiable seller reputation into a single transaction flow that fits how social commerce actually happens.

§ 03

The product, in one page

DealsGate sits between a seller and a buyer. It does not list items. It does not compete with the seller's Instagram storefront. It provides the four things social commerce cannot provide on its own:

01 · Regulated escrow

The buyer's money is held by a licensed Nigerian payment partner. It is not released to the seller until the buyer has seen the inspection report and received the item.

02 · Certified Vet Agents

Every item is physically inspected by a certified, ID-verified field agent before money moves. IMEI, serial, condition, and quality are verified against the seller's listing with continuous video evidence.

03 · Tracked logistics

Dispatch through partner couriers with shared tracking. Buyer and seller see the exact same journey — same states, same timestamps, same webhooks.

04 · Public reputation

Every seller has a public dossier showing verified identity, deal counts, success rate, and buyer reviews. No login required to view it.

The protected deal, in seven steps

  1. Seller creates a deal. Item details, 2–4 photos, a continuous video ≤ 60 seconds, a Google Maps pin, and a ₦5,000 guarantee hold.
  2. Buyer pays into escrow. Item price plus buyer fee, Vet Agent fee, and logistics — held by a licensed payment partner.
  3. Vet Agent accepts and inspects. First certified agent in the seller's LGA accepts the job, travels to the pin, and verifies the item.
  4. Buyer reviews the report. The evidence gate blocks logistics until the buyer approves — or disputes.
  5. Courier dispatches. Shared tracking opens. Both parties watch the same journey.
  6. Item delivered. Buyer has 72 hours to confirm or raise a dispute.
  7. Settlement. Seller receives net payout plus guarantee refund. Ratings update both parties' scores.

What makes it defensible

  • The evidence gate. Logistics cannot pick up the item until the buyer approves the inspection report. This single rule aligns every incentive.
  • Agent paid on approval, not on inspection. Vet Agents are paid when the buyer approves, not when they submit. Nobody is rewarded for cutting corners.
  • Failure paths designed before success paths. Failed inspections, transit damage, fraud, and cancellation all have explicit, pre-published policies — not case-by-case judgement calls.
  • The audit is not optional. Every state transition, every money movement, every admin action carries a durable, timestamped, IP-logged trace.

§ 05

The four invariants

The bundle is built on four rules that never break. They are enforced server-side in the master spec, and they are visibly demonstrated in the demos. If any of them were violated, the platform would collapse.

01

Seller never holds buyer principal

The buyer's money is held exclusively by a licensed Nigerian payment or escrow partner. The platform never takes custody of client funds directly. This is a regulatory requirement, not a design preference — and it is enforced before any deal can be created.

§1.1 Design principles · §15 Wallets · §23 Security · §24 Tech stack
02

Logistics cannot move until the buyer approves evidence

The Vet Agent's inspection report is a gate, not a notification. Neither the item nor the money moves until the buyer has reviewed the report and clicked Approve. A dispute blocks pickup and routes to mediation. This is the single most important buyer protection in the platform.

§10 Evidence gate · §11 Logistics · §21 State machine
03

Verification is a hard gate, not a soft warning

No create-deal, no accept-deal payment, no agent job, and no withdrawal is permitted until verification_status = verified. Policy consent is likewise mandatory — checkboxes are never pre-ticked, and acceptance is persisted with policy_key, policy_version, accepted_at, IP, and user agent.

§4.1 Consent · §4.3 ID gate · §26 Acceptance tests
04

The platform logistics margin is never shown to the buyer

The buyer sees exactly four cost lines: item price, buyer service fee, Vet Agent fee, and logistics (the courier's price). The margin the platform makes on logistics is computed and stored backend-only. It is never a visible line item — not in the checkout, not on the receipt, not in the buyer's wallet ledger.

§8 Checkout · §22 Fees · §26 Acceptance tests
Why these four
Every other feature in the MVP is additive. These four are load-bearing. Remove any one of them and the platform either becomes unsafe for buyers, unfair to sellers, unregulated to operate, or dishonest to the market.

§ 08

Design decisions made during the build

The spec defines what to build. These are the interpretation choices made while building the demos — worth documenting so future implementers understand the reasoning, not just the outcome.

01 · Every demo is one self-contained HTML file

No build step, no bundler, no framework, no external CSS or JavaScript. Each file opens directly in a browser and runs. This makes the bundle trivially portable — it can be emailed, uploaded to any static host, or opened offline. It also makes the demos easy to diff against each other when the spec evolves.

02 · A shared "scenario runner" pattern across all demos

Every demo follows the same interaction contract: a scenario picker, a ▶ Play button, a step list you can tap individually, a narration card that explains what just happened, and a toast for confirmations. This means a reviewer who has learned to use one demo can immediately use all eleven without re-reading instructions.

03 · "Mark done" checkpoints, not auto-advance

The runthrough does not assume you completed an act just because you opened the demo. You tick the checkbox. This respects the reviewer's judgement and preserves their progress across sessions via localStorage.

04 · The buyer's view never shows the margin line

This is the most subtle invariant in the bundle. The checkout shows four lines: item price, buyer service fee, Vet Agent fee, and logistics. It never shows the platform's logistics margin. The buyer · first scenario calls this out explicitly, and the master spec §8 and §22 both require it.

05 · Webhooks are first-class citizens in tracking

The shared-tracking demo does not simulate state changes with timers. Every transition comes from a "partner webhook" that carries a signature, an idempotency key, and structured metadata. This mirrors how the real system will behave and makes the tracking demo an honest reference for engineering.

06 · The agent is paid on buyer approval, not on submission

This is an incentive-design choice. If agents were paid on submission, there would be a subtle pressure to mark items PASS regardless of condition. By tying payment to buyer approval, the agent's interest aligns with the buyer's. If the buyer disputes, the agent still gets paid — but only after mediation confirms the work was honest.

07 · The guarantee is a hold, not a fee

Sellers often misunderstand the ₦5,000 guarantee as a cost of doing business. It is not. It is a temporary hold that returns to the seller's guarantee wallet on every successful completion. Only two paths forfeit it: a failed inspection with material seller fault, or a fraud ruling. Every other outcome refunds it in full.

08 · Admin does not assign jobs on the happy path

The platform opens jobs to the eligible agent pool in the seller's LGA. The first certified agent to accept wins. Admins intervene only on exceptions — overdue jobs, disputes, fraud investigations. This is a deliberate scaling choice: manual assignment does not survive growth.

09 · Policy consent is a first-class feature, not a modal

The auth demo dedicates a full tab to showing exactly what is stored when a user accepts a policy: policy_key, policy_version, accepted_at, IP, user agent. This is not decorative. It is the evidence base for any regulatory audit.

10 · The runthrough is the on-ramp, not the product

A reviewer opening the bundle for the first time should not have to read a spec to know what to look at. The runthrough gives them a story — one deal, eleven acts, one continuous journey. The individual demos are the depth.

§ 10

How to run the bundle

Setup

  1. Copy all twelve HTML files into a single folder.
  2. No build step, no dependencies, no server required.
  3. Open dealsgate-index.html in a modern browser.

Recommended tour

  1. Start at dealsgate-runthrough.html for the full story in eleven acts.
  2. Or open dealsgate-platform.html for the 30-second overview — all users running in parallel.
  3. Then dive into specific demos for depth: Auth → Seller → Buyer → Agent → Admin.
  4. Open each demo, pick a scenario, press ▶ Play, and watch.

Interaction patterns (identical across all demos)

  • ▶ Play in the header runs the currently selected scenario end to end.
  • Scenario list at the top of the Flow tab switches between different stories.
  • Step list below the narration lets you tap any single step to jump to it.
  • Reset clears workflow state but keeps the audit log.
  • Reset all wipes everything including the audit log.
  • Bottom tabs switch between the flow, ledgers, wallets, consents, and audit.

Printing

This brief has a print stylesheet. Press 🖨 Print in the top navigation to produce a clean PDF suitable for circulation.

Browser support

Tested in Chrome, Safari, Firefox, and Edge on both desktop and mobile. Requires no JavaScript features beyond ES2018 and localStorage.

§ 11

What is not in this bundle

Out of scope, by explicit decision in §27 of the master spec. Nothing in this list should be inferred as missing — each was reviewed and deferred.

Out of scope for the MVP

  • Full public product catalogue — DealsGate is not a marketplace. Sellers share direct buyer links, not listings that strangers browse.
  • In-house escrow account — client money is only ever held by licensed payment partners, never by the platform directly.
  • Smart-contract NGN custody — not a launch blocker; explicitly deferred.
  • Zero-knowledge KYC — not a launch blocker; explicitly deferred.
  • Any feature not listed in the FINAL 1.0 spec — requires written approval before being scoped.

Runtime concerns (not demo concerns)

These items from §25 milestone 16 are engineering tasks, not demo tasks. They cannot be shown in a browser demo because they are code and infrastructure, not user surfaces.

  • Automated test suites (unit, integration, end-to-end)
  • Payment provider integration with real Paystack / Flutterwave keys
  • Logistics partner API integrations with live webhook signatures
  • KYC provider integration (NIN, BVN, CAC)
  • Google Maps JavaScript + Places integration
  • Postgres schema, migrations, and index strategy
  • S3-compatible object storage with signed URLs
  • Deployment, monitoring, alerting, observability

The demos describe exactly what these integrations must produce. They do not themselves integrate with third parties. That distinction is important — the demo bundle is a specification artifact, not a production build.

§ 12

Next steps toward production

The demo bundle closes the question "what should we build". The next phase is "how do we build it in production". This section maps the road ahead.

Phase 1 — Engineering foundation (4–6 weeks)

  • Set up the Next.js / React + TypeScript monorepo with the design system extracted from these demos.
  • Stand up Postgres with the §20 data model. Migrations, seed scripts, and the row-lock strategy for first-accept-wins.
  • Wire the authentication layer (email + phone OTP, Google OAuth, TOTP). Implement the ID verification gate server-side.
  • Integrate the first KYC provider (NIN, BVN, CAC) in test mode.

Phase 2 — Money rails (4–6 weeks)

  • Paystack / Flutterwave integration with escrow pattern, verified webhooks, and idempotency keys.
  • Wallet buckets: main, guarantee, agent earnings. Full ledger with double-entry.
  • Expanded Nigerian bank list, beneficiary management, name enquiry.
  • Withdrawal pipeline with step-up 2FA and beneficiary cooling.

Phase 3 — Deal lifecycle (4–6 weeks)

  • Create-deal wizard with all §7.1 validations enforced server-side.
  • Media pipeline: S3-compatible storage, server-side image count and video duration checks, signed URLs.
  • Google Maps + Places integration for inspection pins.
  • Buyer checkout, escrow funding, evidence gate, dispute routing.

Phase 4 — Vet Agents (4 weeks)

  • Agent onboarding, sector selection, service LGAs, bank account capture.
  • Training modules CMS with quiz and certification.
  • LGA job feed with atomic first-accept-wins via row-level lock.
  • GPS verification within 500m of pin, enforced server-side.
  • Inspection form with checklist, media capture, verdicts, earnings.

Phase 5 — Logistics & tracking (3–4 weeks)

  • Partner API integration (GIG, Kwik, or equivalent).
  • Webhook ingress with HMAC verification and idempotency key store.
  • Shared tracking timeline. Buyer and seller both read the same source of truth.
  • SLA timers, auto-complete windows, dispute routing on delivery.

Phase 6 — Admin & content (4 weeks)

  • Admin console with role-based access: viewer, finance, mediator, ops, super admin.
  • Dispute mediation workspace with evidence tiers.
  • Taxonomy CMS, training CMS, fee configuration with dual control.
  • Immutable audit log with actor, IP, before-state, after-state.
  • Public blog, policy library, About, Contact — rendered from CMS.

Phase 7 — Hardening & launch (4–6 weeks)

  • Automated test suites — every acceptance test in §26 becomes a CI gate.
  • Load testing against realistic LGA concurrency.
  • Security review: input validation, authorization boundaries, secret management.
  • Observability: structured logs, distributed tracing, alerting.
  • Soft launch in Lagos, then Ibadan, then Abuja. Expand agent coverage by LGA before expanding deals.
The single most important thing
Agent coverage comes first. A protected deal that cannot be inspected does not exist. Recruit and certify Vet Agents in a city before opening that city to sellers.
§ 13

Appendix — raw spec sections

The verbatim requirements from the master spec that most directly shaped the build. Quoted here so the brief is self-contained for readers who do not have the master spec in front of them.

§1.0 — Core promise

We verify the item, protect the payment, handle logistics, and help buyers and sellers complete transactions safely.

§1.1 — Design principles (excerpt)

Mobile-first, simple, secure. Evidence over opinion (media, checklists, timestamps). Seller never holds buyer principal — regulated third-party payment/escrow only. Platform logistics margin is backend-only; never shown as a buyer line item. One user account; Buyer/Seller is a role on each transaction. Server-side validation, authorization, audit logs, idempotency, verified webhooks. Never store card/payment credentials in the application database.

§4.1 — Mandatory policy consent

Signup cannot complete until user accepts: Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Refund & Dispute Policy (and Acceptable Use if displayed). Checkboxes required and not pre-ticked; links to full policy text. Persist policy_key, policy_version, accepted_at, IP, user_agent.

§4.3 — ID verification gate

No create-deal, accept-deal payment, agent jobs, or withdrawals until verification_status = verified.

§10 — Evidence gate (critical)

After agent submits PASS (or resolved NEEDS_REVIEW), buyer must open report. Buyer: Approve evidence — logistics becomes eligible; OR Dispute — mediation. Logistics MUST NOT pick up until Approve.

§11 — Logistics & shared tracking

After Approval: create shipment via logistics partner API (or simulated partner webhooks). Buyer and seller both see journey: Created → Guarantee → Paid → Agent → Inspected → Evidence approved → Picked up → In transit → Delivered → Completed (or dispute branches). Tracking number/status from partner.

§13.5 — Transit damage — minor

Buyer may claim damage but collect item and complete deal; seller paid; logistics compensates buyer under SLA.

§13.6 — Transit damage — total rejection

Full refund to buyer; seller guarantee refunded; item returned to seller; logistics bears to/from cost and compensates seller for damage.

§23 — Security requirements (excerpt)

Validate all inputs server-side (media count, video duration, quantity, lat/lng, amounts). Authorize every private resource; unguessable buyer-link tokens. Verify payment and logistics webhook signatures. Idempotent payment, refund, payout, guarantee, wallet transfer. Signed URLs for private media; HTTPS; secrets in env. Withdrawal 2FA; beneficiary cooling; rate-limit public search. Public dossier exposes no sensitive PII.

§27 — Out of scope (MVP)

Full public product catalog / SEO marketplace browse. Custom in-house escrow bank account treated as client money without licensed partner. Smart-contract NGN custody or ZKP KYC as launch blockers. Features not listed in this document without written approval.
