1.1The Opportunity

Nigerian social commerce is projected to reach $12.43 billion in 2026 and $20.8 billion by 2031, growing at 13% annually. Roughly 39 million MSMEs operate in Nigeria, and 14 million of them use Meta's platforms as digital storefronts. Yet digital payment fraud cost Nigerians ₦25.85 billion in 2025 across 67,515 reported cases — and the Securities and Exchange Commission found that 80% of Nigerian undergraduates cannot identify basic online scam warning signs.

Every transaction between two strangers on Instagram or WhatsApp runs on blind trust. Buyers fear paying first. Sellers fear shipping first. When fraud happens, there is no practical recourse — bank transfers cannot be reversed, and courts are impractical for daily amounts.

1.2The Product

DealsGate is a protected-deal platform — not a marketplace. It sits underneath peer-to-peer trade and enforces three layers of protection through a single state machine:

Regulated escrow

Buyer's funds held by a licensed Nigerian payment partner. DealsGate never holds client principal.

Physical inspection

A certified Vet Agent physically inspects the item on camera, GPS-verified within 500m of the seller's pin.

The evidence gate

No legal state transition exists from EVIDENCE_REVIEW to LOGISTICS_DISPATCHED except via buyer approval. Enforced server-side.

Shared tracking

Buyer and seller see the same timeline, driven by HMAC-verified courier webhooks. No hidden states.

3.1The Trust Asymmetry

Peer-to-peer trade requires the buyer to release funds before receiving goods and the seller to ship before receiving funds. Neither party can move first without incurring uncompensated risk. When the parties are strangers on Instagram or WhatsApp — with no catalogue, no identity system, and no dispute mechanism — the transaction rationally does not happen.

3.2Buyer Pain
  • Advance-payment fraud. Seller disappears after payment, or ships a different item.
  • No recourse. Bank transfers are irreversible. Court recovery is impractical for ₦50k–₦500k disputes.
  • Fake social proof. Follower counts, screenshots, and testimonials are cheap to fabricate.
  • No item verification. Buyers have no third party confirming the item matches its listing.
3.3Seller Pain
  • Ghost buyers. Order placed, item dispatched, buyer unreachable at delivery.
  • Fake payment alerts. Screenshots of transfers that never happened or were reversed.
  • Disputes with no defence. Buyer claims item differed from listing. Seller has no video, no witness, no record.
  • Lost sales. Serious buyers walk away because they cannot verify seller legitimacy.
3.4Why Existing Solutions Fail

Escrow-only services

Protect the money but not the item. Buyer still receives goods they never saw verified. Payluk, Bridge Escrow, EscrowPay.

Inspection-only services

Verify the item but cannot hold funds. Seller cannot guarantee payment. Fiverr-style task platforms without escrow.

Marketplaces

Require sellers to abandon their own channels and list on a central catalogue. Jumia, Konga, Jiji.

The gap

No platform combines escrow custody with physical inspection and evidence-gated release into one protocol.

The structural problem: money protection and goods protection exist separately. Neither alone closes the trust asymmetry. Combining them in application logic invites bypass. The correct solution is a state machine that makes the bypass structurally impossible.

4.2The Evidence Gate — The Central Invariant

DealsGate's state machine defines 21 legal states (13 main-path + 8 branch) and a closed transition function. Every state change is verified server-side against a whitelist. Illegal transitions are rejected with HTTP 422 before any database write.

The invariant: no edge exists from EVIDENCE_REVIEW to LOGISTICS_DISPATCHED except via APPROVED. This is a structural property of the transition graph, not a UI check.

5.2Security & Integrity
  • Server-side validation on every input — media count, video duration, quantity, lat/lng, amounts
  • Row-level locking for atomic job assignment — SELECT … FOR UPDATE on the job record
  • Partial unique index enforcing one active inspection per agent at the database level
  • Idempotency keys on every payment, refund, payout, guarantee hold, and wallet transfer
  • HMAC-verified webhooks with ±5min replay window for payment and logistics events
  • Signed URLs for all private media · TLS in transit · AES-256 at rest
  • Step-up 2FA on withdrawals — TOTP, Email OTP, or SMS OTP required
  • Role-based access control for admin operations with dual-control on money-critical configuration
5.3Known Technical Risk — Byzantine Agent

The evidence gate assumes the agent's device captures the actual item at the actual location. A rooted or jailbroken device could inject pre-recorded footage or spoof GPS. This is the largest single technical risk in the system. The mitigation path (hardware-backed device attestation via Android Keystore / iOS Secure Enclave, motion co-capture, monotonic server timestamps, structured red-team testing) is scheduled for the production build.

8.2Our Position — Four Differentiators

No other Nigerian platform blocks logistics until the buyer approves a physical inspection report. Escrow protects money; we protect the item too.

02 · Physical verification at scale

Certified Vet Agents inspect every item in person with continuous video as court-grade evidence. A physical network, not a feature toggle.

Buyers verify any vendor's real deal history before paying. No login required. The directory compounds as a network asset.

Failed inspections, transit damage, fraud, cancellation — all have pre-published rules, not case-by-case judgement.

8.3The Structural Moat
Competitors can copy our screens in a week. They cannot copy a certified Vet Agent network across 50 LGAs in under twelve months. The moat is physical and operational — the same class as Uber's driver network, not its app.

9.1The Single Rule
A city does not open for sellers until agent coverage hits the density threshold. One certified agent per 15 expected daily deals. Coverage is not a growth problem — it is the product.
9.2Four Phases
PhaseObjective
Phase 1 · Lagos pilotIkeja · Eti-Osa · Surulere · Kosofe. 50 agents certified. 500 protected deals. First revenue cohort.
Phase 2 · Multi-cityReplicate the LGA playbook in Ibadan, Abuja, Port Harcourt. 200 agents. 5,000 monthly deals.
Phase 3 · Category expansionAdd vehicles and appliances as dedicated inspection verticals. Specialist agents. Higher-value transactions.
Phase 4 · Cross-borderAfCFTA trade corridors. Same escrow + inspection model across intra-African transactions.
9.3Acquisition Channels
  • Vet Agent recruitment: targeted outreach to technicians, phone repairers, gadget communities in each LGA. WhatsApp, church networks, university campuses, existing vendor referrals.
  • Seller onboarding: agents recruit sellers. A certified agent who knows local vendors becomes the acquisition channel for that LGA.
  • Buyer acquisition: sellers bring buyers through shareable deal links. Every link is a protected deal page and a public seller dossier.
  • Public seller directory: searchable without login. Organic SEO on Nigerian vendor names. Viral potential — every vendor shares their dossier link.
  • Referral programme: ₦1,000 per referee's first completed deal. 50% lifetime discount at 10 referrals. Compounding word-of-mouth.
  • Content and social: founder-led narrative on LinkedIn, X, and Instagram. Educational content on safe online trade. Nigerian tech press outreach.
9.4Why Agents Come First

Sellers join a platform that buyers trust. Buyers trust a platform where they know the item will be verified. Verification requires an agent in the LGA. Therefore: agent density is the constraint. Every city launch begins with recruitment, not marketing.

14.1Technical Risk
Byzantine agent. A rooted device can inject pre-recorded video or spoof GPS. Mitigation: hardware-backed device attestation (Android Keystore / iOS Secure Enclave), motion co-capture, monotonic server timestamps, structured red-team testing, and fallback to dual-device signing plus buyer unboxing video as Tier-2 evidence.
14.2Operational Risk
  • Agent density failure. Mitigation: LGA-by-LGA launch. City opens only when agent coverage hits threshold. ₦5,000 completion bonus to seed first 50 agents per city.
  • Fake inspection. Mitigation: continuous video, IMEI-on-camera, GPS verification within 500m, buyer unboxing video as second-tier evidence, mediator arbitration.
  • Logistics partner failure. Mitigation: multi-partner abstraction. GIG and Kwik both integrated. No single-courier dependency.
14.3Regulatory Risk
  • Escrow licensing. Mitigation: DealsGate never holds client principal. All escrow custodied by a licensed payment partner.
  • Data protection. Mitigation: full NDPA 2023-aligned policy library. NDPC registration upon live launch.
  • KYC compliance. Mitigation: NIN, BVN, CAC verification through authorised providers. Raw identity numbers never stored.
14.4Market Risk
  • Adoption. Mitigation: users already pay for safety through workarounds. The product formalises an existing behaviour.
  • Competition. Mitigation: physical agent network is not copiable in under 12 months. First-mover advantage in Lagos and Ibadan.
  • Cash flow. Mitigation: grant funding funds 12 months of operations. Seed follows on proven pilot data.
14.5Contingency
If the pilot fails to reach 100 deals in the first quarter, the strategy pivots to two options: (1) narrow to a single high-density LGA and double down on agent training and seller outreach, or (2) shift to on-demand inspection as the primary product, monetising agent verification before platform-scale escrow.
